The Amalfi Coast: What Nobody Tells You

MAY 5, 2026 · ITALY TRAVEL GUIDE

The Amalfi Coast is one of those rare places that genuinely lives up to the photos. Pastel-coloured villages stacked on cliffs above turquoise water — it's real, and it's as beautiful as you imagine. But most guides skip the practical truths that make or break a trip here.

Best months to visit are May, June, and September. Crowds are manageable, the sea is warm enough to swim, and prices are reasonable. July and August are peak season — hot, packed, and expensive. If your budget allows, spend at least two nights on the coast itself. The mornings and evenings, when the day-trippers have gone, are the best times to experience the towns as they really are.

Our verdict: Positano is the most iconic but you'll pay for the view. Praiano offers the best balance of beauty and value. Ravello sits high above the coast — the views from Villa Cimbrone are some of the most stunning in Italy. Book early, pack light, and come ready to take it slow. That's what this coast rewards.

Days on the Coast — The Perfect Itinerary

APRIL 22, 2026 · ITINERARY

Four to five days is the sweet spot for the Amalfi Coast. Enough time to see the main towns, do a hike, take a boat trip to Capri, and eat well without rushing. Here is how to spend it.

Day 1: Arrive in Positano. Settle into your accommodation, explore the winding boutiques, sunset drinks with a view of the vertical city. Day 2: Boat trip to Capri — Blue Grotto, Piazzetta, and a seafood lunch by the marina. Day 3: Ravello — the gardens of Villa Cimbrone and Villa Rufolo. Day 4: Path of the Gods hike or Pompeii excursion. Day 5: Leisure morning and departure.

The ferry network (April to October) is the best way to move between towns. It's scenic, fast, and lets you avoid the coastal road entirely. Ferries connect Positano, Amalfi, Salerno, and Capri. Buy tickets at the harbour or online for busy routes.
